# Classic Doom visual acceptance audit

The revision needs textured spatial depth and convincing creature and weapon mass. Classic Doom's visible pixel structure sits on top of strong form shading, scale, occlusion and lighting. The rejected eight-ink boards simplify all of those together; adding more surface marks alone will keep the same flat result.

## Reference scope

The primary source is Bethesda's [DOOM + DOOM II Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2280/DOOM__DOOM_II/), which identifies id Software's original 1993 and 1994 games and distinguishes them from the enhanced rerelease and newer included episodes. The downloaded publisher screenshots are current rerelease captures, not verified raw DOS captures. Screenshots 01–06 provide the stock classic visual vocabulary used below. The gallery also contains newer classic-engine content; screenshots 07–12 are context only. None is a Doom Eternal reference. The store's promotional inclusion collage, screenshot 00, is not a gameplay art target.

## What the reference provides

Architecture has textured wall, floor and ceiling planes that recede through space. A lowered liquid pit, wrapping walkway and distant opening establish distinct near, middle and far zones in [screenshot 01](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2280/ss_e46818d3d16bdb6d8f07d0ca23c1dc05d0073b93.1920x1080.jpg?t=1790284857). Visible riser faces, overlapping stairs and a brighter destination doorway create depth in [screenshot 04](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2280/ss_a5e57e91c054298a6c86ac49148225f936101a45.1920x1080.jpg?t=1790284857). Material detail follows those surfaces instead of substituting for their volume.

Creatures have physical masses. In [screenshot 05](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2280/ss_33492406a4fcad478fccd9b8c8ff169d9dece124.1920x1080.jpg?t=1790284857), the large horned figures use shaded chest, abdomen, shoulder and leg forms, overlapping limbs and dark joint recesses. In [screenshot 06](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2280/ss_3714172f6c26be6aacd532e550fd11a6fe02063d.1920x1080.jpg?t=1790284857), broad bodies and heavy paired weapons read at a different scale from the distant creatures. This supports an art inference: the desired threat must carry weight through anatomy and pose before ornamental horns, cables or glowing features are added.

Weapon views establish a substantial object held into depth. The centered pistol in [screenshot 02](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2280/ss_806877de2ec13666c33ec2eed02055585543f23f.1920x1080.jpg?t=1790284857) has visible barrel, receiver, grip and hand volumes. The shotgun reload in [screenshot 03](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2280/ss_75eb1d7b9d343c47fa352bc66a065b006b1b2002.1920x1080.jpg?t=1790284857) changes its pose while retaining material highlights and foreshortening. Bright muzzle flashes and projectiles punctuate darker, textured rooms rather than every room surface sharing the same visual emphasis.

## What failed in the rejected boards

The three inspected boards in `visual/` use almost the same frontal corridor, large even-color wall areas and similar illumination across every plane. Their enemies rely on narrow limbs, contour edges and graphic symbols; chest, pelvis, joints and weight transfer have little modeled volume. Their held guns appear as oblique, flat, small housings with decorative marks, so they carry less mechanical depth than the classic centered barrel views. The palette restriction is a contributor, but loosening it must serve shading and material hierarchy rather than add arbitrary colors.

## Criteria for the next review

These are proposed practical gates, not historical Doom specifications. Review original enemy poses on transparent backgrounds at 128–256 pixels tall, then in the actual Godot room at **640 × 360**, without browser enlargement.

1. **Visible room depth.** The gameplay camera must show three overlapping spatial zones and at least two floor elevations or a visible lowered pit. Steps need visible risers; a framed opening must reveal another space. Moving laterally must produce parallax and correct occlusion between a near column, middle threat and far doorway.
2. **Form shading.** Each major creature mass must have coherent shadow, half-tone and highlight placement, with roughly four to six deliberate tonal steps available within its dominant material. At 128 pixels tall, a chest or shell must read as curved volume, and a joint or cavity must read as recessed. A uniform fill with a dark outline fails even if the silhouette is recognizable.
3. **Anatomy and weight.** The head, torso/pelvis and attacking limb or weapon must separate through overlap and shading. Grounded threats need feet under a plausible center of mass; heavy threats need broad support, substantial limbs and believable weapon attachment. Check the feet against the floor from the actual camera. Thin stick limbs or a floating cutout without contact fail.
4. **Threat scale and recognition.** Render the same sprite at approximately 128, 64 and 32 pixels tall in the 640 × 360 room. At 64 pixels, a viewer must identify its attack-bearing feature and facing direction. At 32 pixels, the body silhouette must still separate from the background. A heavier enemy must read heavier through body proportions and footprint, not only a different accent color.
5. **Weapon perspective and mass.** The resting muzzle should aim through the central sight line, within roughly 16 pixels horizontally at 640 × 360, with the near receiver wider than the distant barrel. A visible hand, grip, receiver and barrel need distinct shaded planes. The weapon must occupy a substantial part of the lower quarter to third of the frame while leaving the middle threat visible. A side-view icon tipped diagonally toward the screen fails.
6. **Materials and light hierarchy.** Show at least three materially different surfaces whose texture follows perspective and whose brightness follows the room's lighting. Recesses and distant openings must have a darker value family than exposed foreground faces. Reserve the brightest, most saturated accents for threats, projectiles, muzzle flashes or selected fixtures. In a grayscale 640 × 360 view, the target and route must remain identifiable.
7. **Pixel structure at game scale.** Use purposeful clusters and nearest sampling for creature/weapon sprites; choose environment filtering after observing actual camera motion. Review at native 640 × 360 plus a nearest enlarged view. Reject smeared sprite edges, isolated noisy pixels that obscure anatomy, flat tiles stretched across large surfaces, and a pass that only looks convincing when enlarged as a concept board.

The next review should contain one actual room camera view, the same view in grayscale, near/middle/far enemy placements, and a resting weapon view. This is enough to assess the template without producing full animation sheets or selecting an identity.
