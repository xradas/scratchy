# Creature redesign research and production direction

Research date: 3 October 2026. Status: a replacement design specification for critique; no new production sprites or gameplay changes are claimed. This document replaces the creative direction of the Kiln Wretch proposal rather than extending that creature. Numerical values below are proposed tuning targets, not findings about the current game or Doom.

## What the reference work actually shows

The useful inheritance from Doom is an encounter vocabulary: enemies ask for different player decisions. The original AI separates line attacks, simple missiles, charging skulls, close attacks, projectile spreads, resurrection and boss attacks. Former humans make exposure costly; Imps teach dodging; Demons take space; Cacodemons occupy air; Revenants complicate movement with tracking missiles; Mancubi spread fire; Arachnotrons sustain pressure; Arch-viles make cover and target priority urgent. Those are functional interpretations of the behaviors, not official taxonomy labels. Copy the decision contrasts, then give Eyesore original anatomy, materials, movement and attacks. Source: [id Software, p_enemy.c](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM/blob/master/linuxdoom-1.10/p_enemy.c).

Doom's state definitions attach actions and durations to individual animation states, with separate chase, attack, pain and death sequences. Sprite animation and attack timing therefore share a contract; having four pictures called “attack” does not establish that contract. Source: [id Software, info.c](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM/blob/master/linuxdoom-1.10/info.c).

The original renderer supports eight rotation slots for directional frames, optional flipped images, patch offsets and all-angle frames. A billboard is the display primitive; the selection of a view still has to express the actor's facing. Source: [id Software, r_things.c](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM/blob/master/linuxdoom-1.10/r_things.c).

Boltgun's developers describe a concrete asset method: model, texture, rig and animate enemies, render those animations from eight virtual camera directions, then assemble the images into flipbooks. Per-enemy animation data specifies damage, sound and projectile moments. Its state machine includes awareness, flinch, death and overkill. This is stronger production evidence than assuming a chunky screenshot came from primitive low-poly character geometry: the rendered result is sprite-based, while its source art uses a modern rigged-model workflow. [Auroch Digital developer explanation on PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/04/11/warhammer-40-000-boltgun-releases-may-23-new-gameplay-details-revealed/).

Proposed application: use an approved original rigged sculpt/blockout as the consistent camera, skeleton and timing source, then render at the fixed logical resolution and perform pixel cleanup. Hand-authored sprites are still viable, but the same turntable, pivot and pose standards apply. A repeated generated character sheet has no comparable guarantee of anatomy or timing. The first route trial should compare one rig-rendered/cleaned pose and one hand-authored pose at 48 px/m in game before choosing the production method.

## Current project audit

Inspected `linux-game/src/engine3d.cpp`, both enemy frame builders, `docs/SPRITE_PRODUCTION_BRIEF.md`, prototype notes, `enemy-0-directional-source.png` and `enemy-2-combat-source.png`. These are direct local observations:

1. **The roster is visually overcompressed.** The inspected walk source is a glossy red/black armoured humanoid with horns, glowing face, round shoulder plates and many small highlights. The inspected floating caster repeats dark armour, a red face and abundant orange fire. These shapes have individually detailed surfaces but insufficient separation of body plans, weight, material and attack function. This is an art assessment from those sources, not a claim that all four sources were individually inspected.
2. **Combat ignores directional views.** `enemy_model()` selects a direction only for the directional atlas. Pain, attack, death and gib select `combat_frames[variant][row][column]` without a direction dimension. The actor can turn from its side walk into a front-facing attack while still aiming elsewhere. This is a confirmed code limitation.
3. **Every enemy shares the same temporal body language.** The four classes use the same four-frame walk and combat clips, with the same attack event frame. Ranged/melee differences are largely indexed by type. A bigger sprite has no uniquely weighted wind-up or recovery merely because it is larger.
4. **Alpha is destroyed and reconstructed.** Both builders flatten enemy output onto black. The loader marks pixels transparent when every RGB component is below 12. This can remove legitimate dark interior/outline pixels and contaminate antialiased edges with black. Some source backgrounds are removed by a neutral-color heuristic and morphology; pale neutral creature material could be erased by that heuristic.
5. **Placement is inferred from each image.** The builder trims and south-centres cells. A detached particle, wide attack, changing lean or asymmetrical limb can change the bounding box and move the implied body anchor. A special fourth cultist row is additionally resized to 230 pixels. A common canvas does not establish a common skeletal pivot.
6. **Source grids have fractional boundaries.** Existing brief records 1774×887 directional sheets, which do not divide evenly into 8×4. Equal crop commands and type-specific repairs are compensating for unsuitable masters.
7. **Sprite lighting has a confirmed implementation defect.** `enemy_model()` computes regional brightness and sets `glColor4f(sprite_light,...)`, but `sprite_plane()` immediately replaces it with `glColor4f(1,1,1,alpha)`. The requested regional brightness is therefore overwritten. Enemy drawing also disables fixed-function lighting. Brightness computation alone will not make new sprites inhabit the level.
8. **Scale contracts assume similar tall bodies.** All four definitions are approximately 2.05–2.55 world units tall; a built-in check rejects an enemy shorter than the player. That excludes small fodder and low rushing forms. Canvas width is inferred as 1.5 times height. Collision is independently defined, which is useful, but new body plans require authored dimensions and pivots.

The present work supports a full redesign: cleaner source masters, different bodies and behaviors, directional combat, class-specific animation, and a lighting contract. Repainting the current horned armour alone would preserve most of the problems.

## Three complete art routes

| Route | World / body language | Materials and palette | Strength / cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — The Glass Choir (recommended)** | An abandoned optical and anatomical institute has grown an organism that measures, records and replaces living bodies. Apertures, bellows, tension cables and surgical fixtures recur. Small enemies are loose tissue or orderlies; large enemies are moving apparatus. | Chalk tissue, petrol-blue iron, smoked glass, tarnished brass; small amber danger apertures and bruised violet interiors. Wet material has sparse hard glints; cloth is broad matte planes. | Strong title connection to Eyesore, distinct non-horned silhouettes, rich regional escalation. Glass must be stylized as solid dark facets with highlights; widespread translucency would harm readability. |
| B — The Tidal Ossuary | A drowned coastal pumping city with parasitic reef organisms occupying diving gear, turbines and pressure vessels. Bodies are squat crustaceans, hanging sacs and heavy walkers. | Salt white, deep indigo, copper oxide, desaturated flesh, small coral threat accents. | Excellent variation and audio identity; water rendering and submerged navigation would expand technical scope. No requirement to make every enemy aquatic. |
| C — The Broken Pageant | Monumental civic statues, parade machinery and votive effigies wake under a ruined city. Articulated stone ribs and cloth banners advertise attack poses. | Warm limestone, black lacquer, dried crimson cloth, dull gold, sparse ivory sparks. | Most graphic silhouettes and clean pixel clusters; stone enemies need strong fracture/death variation to avoid uniform hit feedback. |

Recommend A. It gives enemies a shared origin without making all of them humanoid. The same material kit supports sterile wards, flooded archives, machinery halls, exposure gardens and an observatory. Avoid literal giant eyeballs on every creature: the shared motif is a mechanism that opens, focuses or listens, expressed differently by each class.

Wolfenstein and Boltgun sharpen the route choice. Route A supports disciplined pale-uniform personnel versus mechanical apparatus versus loose biological forms, keeping a clear faction/body hierarchy. Route B gets especially strong organic/armoured contrast but adds water and translucent-material risks. Route C inherits Boltgun's useful monumental architecture/body scale contrast, but its gold, stone and heavy silhouettes need deliberate distance from Warhammer visual motifs. In all routes, borrow Wolfenstein's decisive state change and Boltgun's authored volumetric sprite production, while designing original equipment, symbols and anatomy.

## Recommended art contract

All values here are proposals for a new content path. They intentionally require a data-driven renderer rather than conforming new designs to the current fixed arrays.

- **Logical density:** 48 authored pixels per metre at the model's scale. Standard logical canvas 192×128; engine compatibility export 384×256 at exact nearest-neighbour 2×. Small 0.65 m forms occupy approximately 31 logical pixels in height; a 1.8 m humanoid approximately 86; a 2.4 m anchor approximately 115. Do not make every body fill its cell. Surveyor uses a 384×256 logical canvas, exported to 768×512; the 6 m Parallax Choir uses 512×384, exported to 1024×768, at the same density. Wider attack poses can require a larger fixed canvas while retaining density.
- **Value masses:** ordinary bodies use three broad value groups before detail: dark structure, middle material, pale face/tool/body plane. At least one uninterrupted pale or midlight body patch is visible from every view. Ambient level surfaces should generally be quieter and less contrasty behind active lanes.
- **Palette:** near-black petrol `#182328`; structural blue `#334C55`; worn blue `#677C7B`; tissue shadow `#806D79`; tissue mid `#B7A39C`; chalk `#E3D7BE`; brass shadow `#66533A`; brass `#A58448`; amber tell `#F4C66A`; threat red `#CB594A`; violet interior `#754C91`; spark ivory `#FFF0CE`. These are authoring swatches, not an automatic quantization filter. Use approximately 16–24 chosen colors per creature including intermediate ramps, with broad clusters rather than dithering over every surface.
- **Material rules:** tissue has irregular broad planes and one wet seam; metal has hard planar edges and short glints; fabric has two or three wide folds; glass has a mostly opaque dark body with a pale rim and one refracted interior patch. Ordinary enemies have no permanent flame halos.
- **Shape grammar:** fragile targets are narrow or open; pressure enemies extend horizontally; ranged threats deploy an obvious projecting tool; heavy anchors have wide low centres of mass; support enemies create a tall open frame; bosses combine several established mechanisms at architectural size.
- **Animation:** holds establish weight; changes of silhouette establish intent. Animate centre of mass, foot contact, recoil and recovery first. Texture flicker, random wounds and shifting greebles are defects. An attack must still be recognizable in a solid one-color silhouette.

`8×N` means N genuinely authored poses in each of eight directions. The standard direction order is front, front-right, right, back-right, back, back-left, left, front-left. Rotation order is evaluated relative to actor facing. Do not mirror asymmetric weapons or anatomy to save directions.

Every listed sequence gets all eight directions, including combat and pain. Boss direction counts may later increase to 16 only if the orbiting player exposes unacceptable angle stepping; the minimum production budget is eight. Do not author hundreds of frames until the front/side/back body and one full attack are approved in the actual renderer.

Use authored frame durations, not a global frame rate: locomotion typically 90–140 ms per pose, fast release frames 40–70 ms, wind-up holds and recoveries deliberately longer. The roster table controls whole-action timing. An event occurs on the first release pose; socket position and aim are sampled at that moment. Interruptions clear queued release events. Pain immunity windows and stagger thresholds are per class so rapid fire does not permanently freeze heavy enemies.

## Introduction, escalation and encounter breadth

The proposed foundation is 14 original body plans, of which 12 are ordinary enemies and two bosses. Add ten meaningful variants for 24 encounter entries; variation changes an attack, movement constraint or environmental use and must add a visible attachment/body change. Recolor-only difficulty variants do not count as new content.

| Stage | Teach / combine | Later variant and visual delta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake ward | Suture alone, then Stapler behind a low barrier; player sees a shot safely hit cover. | **Splint Suture:** one long leg brace, slower approach but longer hook. **Drum Stapler:** large drum magazine, five-shot burst and longer reload. |

| Archive galleries | Threader introduced with obvious pillars, then a Tripod Engine defines a lane while fodder occupies cross routes. | **Double-Spool Threader:** two visible spools, sequential darts with a longer reset. **Shielded Tripod:** a low front shield, weak leg housings visible from both sides. |
| Compression works | Compression Saint across a broad room; Lens Marshal later advances from a flank. | **Vented Saint:** back chimney and side vents, shorter fan but lingering puddle with clear floor outline. **Cleaver Marshal:** split blade and missing shield corner, faster melee at lower HP. |

| Observatory / deep core | Surveyor revisits cover, aiming and recovery; Parallax Choir recombines learned lanes and pressure. | Boss escalation changes sequencing and available space; avoid hidden numerical speed boosts. |

At first introduction, use one new behavior and an uncomplicated backdrop. At second appearance, pair with a known behavior. At third, alter height, distance or route choice. Specific recipes: Stapler + Rasp means cover must be moved between; Threader + Tripod means cover must be crossed at the vent; Nurse + Sutures means priority changes without raising an old heavy behind the player. Avoid simultaneously teaching tracking, resurrection and aerial charging.

## Lighting, level and FX agreements

**Enemy lighting:** start with authored broad material shading plus a per-actor neutral luminance and restrained scene tint. Evaluate the light near head and torso, not only floor position. Expose one controlled neutral readability floor in gameplay lighting, separately from optional dark showcase art. Correct the current `sprite_plane()` color overwrite before evaluating any lighting proposal. A future normal-map sprite pass is an option, not a prerequisite; incorrect normals per direction would create worse volume cues.

**Concrete tint remedy:** add explicit `tint_r`, `tint_g`, `tint_b` parameters to `sprite_plane()` and set `glColor4f(tint_r,tint_g,tint_b,alpha)` there once; pass the actor's computed lighting from `enemy_model()` and explicit white from projectile callers that require it. Alternatively remove color-setting from the helper and require every caller to set color, but the explicit-parameter version makes state ownership easier to audit. Restore GL color to white after the draw. First validate fixed 1.0 and 0.55 neutral factors on the same stationary enemy/camera and opaque midtone pixels; then validate a red/green tint. A changed variable in the debugger does not pass unless framebuffer pixels change accordingly. This is a specified fix and validation procedure, not a claim that code has already been changed here.

**Colored light:** concept review must show neutral, dark blue, red warning and green archive lighting. A pale torso/tool plane and dark internal gap remain separable even when accent hues converge. Attack recognition uses posture, geometry, time and sound. Amber emissive pixels are restrained, with an explicit mask; they do not illuminate every contour. No enemy class is identified only by red versus green.

**Level geometry:** provide height bands suited to each shape. Ground fodder occupies the floor, Threader/Tender use reachable ledges, rays cross voids visible against sky, heavy anchors need at least their collision width plus a player escape margin. Do not put a 2.2 m wide anchor in a doorway narrower than its actual body. A low-ceiling room excludes Rays or uses a deliberate grounded version. Enemy overhang art must not be mistaken for traversable collision.

**Texture relationship:** active backdrops use broad wall bays and restrained material detail around enemy head height. Floor patterns indicate ledges and hazard extents but avoid high-contrast crawling noise beneath Ticks. Skybox luminance is checked behind all flight silhouettes. Distinct floor, wall, ceiling/sky and elevation trim categories must be present in level material metadata.

**Weapon and FX separation:** player guns occupy lower central space with mostly desaturated warm metal; hostile projectiles use solid closed diamond/glob/needle forms with a dark border and pale core. Player arc fire uses a thin segmented line with an open forked end. Blood is broad dark red fragments; sparks are short ivory strokes. These geometry differences survive color lighting. Do not use the same starburst for hit, cast, muzzle and death. Put launch FX in separate layers so a body's pivot cannot move when its flare expands. Ordinary hit sparks last roughly 60–120 ms; opaque portions must not obscure more than one quarter of the target silhouette during the next critical tell. This is a proposed review limit, measured from captured frames.

**Audio handoff:** each family gets a material signature: soft stitch/cloth for Suture, stapler mechanism for Stapler, glass tick/air tone for Tender, rough jaw scrape for Rasp, cloth click for Tick, bellows breath for Ray, high glass whistle for Mote, spool rattle for Threader, brace lock for Tripod, pressure valve for Saint, plate drag for Marshal and distinct multi-part inhale for Nurse. One unmistakable preattack cue per attack; optional idle layers must not resemble those cues. Sound onset, release and recovery markers belong in the same clip metadata as the sprite events.

## Export and runtime contract

1. Author and approve a turnaround with actual front, quarter, side and back shape relationships. A sheet generated in one pass is source reference until anatomy and scale agree across views. Preserve an editable layered master and palette; keep generated source and approved pixels distinct.
2. Use an exact integer cell grid. Standard logical cell 192×128; floor pivot `(96,120)` in top-left pixel coordinates. The bottom eight logical pixels are reserved for floor effects/contact tolerance. Feet attach to this pivot in every grounded state; airborne pivot is projected ground centre and altitude is metadata. Surveyor pivot is `(192,240)` on 384×256 logical cells; Parallax Choir pivot is `(256,360)` on 512×384 logical cells. At 48 px/m its 6 m body needs 288 logical vertical pixels, which does not fit the smaller boss cell.
3. Never trim, auto-centre, independently resize or infer pivots from opaque bounds. Wide poses may use a larger fixed canvas for the whole creature, keeping 48 px/m and the documented pivot. The renderer accepts per-creature canvas dimensions and world scale.
4. Export RGBA PNG masters, binary alpha for body art, and a separate emissive/FX mask. Semitransparent FX are separate sprites. Native BMP export must preserve explicit alpha with a supported 32-bit format; remove black-key reconstruction for the new path. Black tissue shadows remain opaque. Export orientation, channel order and alpha behavior are verified using actual SDL-loaded texture pixels.
5. Directional file schema: `creatures/{id}/{clip}/d{0..7}/f{000..N}.png`. Native export mirrors it. Metadata includes ID, version, palette, canvas pixels, logical pixels/metre, pivot pixels, visible physical size, collision shape, baseline/altitude, direction order, frame durations, events, socket coordinates, emissive mask and corpse state. Atlas packing may change storage later without changing content IDs.
6. Events include `tell_sound`, `footstep`, `release_projectile` or `hitscan`, `impact`, `recovery_end`, `death_settle`. Every event is fired at most once despite a long simulation step. Visual pose selection does not alter collision opportunistically; attack hit volumes have explicit time windows.
7. Replace fixed `[4]` enemy arrays and fixed four-frame clip assumptions before loading this roster. Use named IDs and metadata. Preserve actual alpha and multiply neutral light/scene tint once inside the draw call. This is planned engine work; this document has not implemented it.

## Production acceptance gates

**Gate 1: roster direction.** Show all 14 base bodies in one silhouette lineup at the proposed world sizes, plus front/side/back for the first four and each route's environment strip. Review at typical enemy display sizes of 24, 48, 96 and 192 screen pixels tall. The first four roles must be distinguishable without labels, highlights or sound.

**Gate 2: one finished regular enemy.** Suture or Tender gets a fully authored walk plus attack/pain/death/gib in all eight views. Inspect animation at 1× logical scale and in the actual game. Reject repeated poses, breathing scale, swapped weapons, drifting faces and ground sliding. It must pass before producing the other full sets.

**Gate 3: directional combat in actual game pixels.** In a fixed camera orbit, capture front, quarter, side and rear at wind-up, release, pain and death. When an enemy attacks another actor during infighting, the player must see the correct side/back attack; firing socket and projectile direction must agree with it. Compare to a compass and world-space facing debug marker. A side-walk/front-attack snap fails regardless of atlas frame count.

**Gate 4: alpha and pivot in actual game pixels.** Load a swatch containing opaque RGB `(0,0,0)`, `(8,8,8)`, `(12,12,12)`, pale neutral pixels, alpha 0 and opaque edge pixels through the production loader. Capture on white, middle gray and black walls: opaque black must stay solid, neutral tissue must remain present, transparent pixels must reveal the wall, and no black/white fringe may surround the silhouette. Repeat with final body frames at 24/48/96 px heights and at fractional camera distances. Compare foot contact with a marked floor grid through all directions and states; attack particles may not move the body pivot.

**Gate 5: lighting and combat clarity.** Capture every base creature against each intended material family under neutral, red, green and dark blue conditions. Repeat while player weapon fires and a neighboring enemy attacks. Confirm the regional light really affects the sprite after the color-overwrite fix. The tell silhouette and projectile must survive the busiest approved scene. Evaluate the floor shadow separately so it never becomes fake collision.

**Gate 6: encounter integration.** Play introduction, mixed encounter and height variation with each new role. Review player escape lanes, true collision size, surprise damage and audio priority. No production completion claim is based only on dimension/count scripts or a pretty contact sheet. Evidence consists of captured game views, animation loops, short sound/attack timing clips and named corrections.

## Cross-discipline critique agenda

The design lead should challenge three assumptions before production: whether optical/anatomical imagery fits the wider level direction; whether the first six enemy shapes read at the actual renderer resolution; and whether the engine can fund directional combat plus altitude before content expands. Level design should return specific encounter lanes and ceiling heights for Rasp, Ray and Saint. Lighting should return one difficult material/light pairing per family. Audio should return tell durations it can make distinct in simultaneous combat. Creature design then revises anatomy, timings or palettes in response and records decisions here.

Open decision: standard eight directions is recommended for the first production pass; more directions are expensive and do not solve weak silhouettes. Open implementation dependency: new frame metadata, explicit alpha and directional combat must land together. Open art question: validate 48 px/m in the actual game before finalizing thousands of frames. The proposed 24 entries represent breadth with deliberate escalation; they are not delivered assets, and no “20×” improvement is claimed by counting files.
