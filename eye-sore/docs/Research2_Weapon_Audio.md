## Decision to put before the team

The next useful deliverable is a small, new-source combat audition with an explicit player decision, not another batch of the rejected sound recipe. Compare a dry physical control against **Struck Mass** (compact percussion and crushing material) and **Fractured Light** (brittle fracture, electrostatic discharge and a short resonant cavity). Both must first win on an ordinary close-range spread weapon. Only the winning identity expands to precision and area weapons. These names describe alternative source grammars; they do not decide the story, gun mechanics or art.

The current output format is adequate for SFX. Source selection, damaged playback transients, repeated material vocabulary and incomplete event semantics are the demonstrated problems. Changing the encoder alone cannot resolve them. Correct the playback audit questions before using gameplay auditions to reject source material.

**Source-build failure:** the legacy recipe trims shotgun recordings from zero for 0.58 seconds. Earlier audit measured first strong attack windows near 0.78, 1.69 and 0.43 seconds; the first two selected regions omit the blast. The later rejected audition instead used 0.785/1.695/0.426 offsets. This explains why more bass/noise processing of some legacy takes was fundamentally misguided, but corrected trimming did not satisfy the user. Preserve both facts.

**Shared processing failure:** all weapons use recorded firearm + metal + sine body + noise, downsample the firearm to 11.025 kHz and upsample, then compress/limit. Shotgun adds 66 Hz exponential sine; pistol 82 Hz; cannon a slowed AK plus 54 Hz. Those recipes mainly change size/pitch while retaining a common source grammar. Default compression attack 2–3 ms can also alter onset. This is an artistic hypothesis to audition, not an automatic claim that compression sounds bad.

**Mixer attack failure:** new `Voice` left/right gains initialize to zero, then approach their target by `/220` per sample. At 44.1 kHz this is about a 5 ms time constant, approximately 63% at 5 ms, 95% at 15 ms. A correctly edited initial transient loses gain. Gain/pan smoothing is useful for an existing source; the same ramp must not automatically soften every new shot. Future comparison: existing ramp versus initial gain set to target with only source-specific click prevention. Do not equate a fix with an artistic pass.

**Mix facts:** 44.1 kHz S16 mono clip buffers; stereo output, 512 requested sample buffer; 24 effect voices with class ceilings 6 weapon/14 combat/4 interface. Constant-power left/right gains, attenuation `1/(1+.075*distance)`, no hard culling distance. Positioned voices keep event X/Z; listener updates move their apparent pan but source motion is not attached. Background stays mono/centered. Master 0.45, effects 0.8 and background 0.22 by default; voice gain up to 1. Output ceiling uses instantaneous downward gain and ~100 ms upward time constant, not a lookahead true-peak limiter. This can suppress later attacks when many tails accumulate. Statistics expose limiting/stealing/drops, but do not prove perceptual success.

**Dispatch facts:** fire cue is local and centered at accepted fire (`engine3d.cpp` around 473), before no future animation marker abstraction. Standard pistol/spread mode emits traveling visual projectiles; calibration mode uses hitscan. On projectile enemy collision, an enemy hit and generic impact both fire; on calibration hitscan enemy damage only enemy hit fires. Calibration surface miss sets a visual impact without playing the generic impact. Enemy attack release uses animation event frame, but there is no matching windup tell. No ammo/reload mechanic currently warrants inventing a reload loop. These divergences can make a good sound seem inconsistent across modes.

**Recording limitation:** current playtest recorder pipes framebuffer RGB to FFmpeg; it does not capture audio. A video file from this path cannot verify audiovisual latency or gameplay mix. Future audition captures require audio loopback synchronized to video or deterministic event-log rendering, labeled according to method.

**Reproducibility gap:** earlier report found recipe output duration vs checked-in files differed and a prototype script root-path issue. Freeze hashes and recipes for the next audition. `verify_audio_assets.sh` checks PCM format/rate/channel and file existence; it does not assess leading silence, missing blasts, mono cancellation, onset timing, material identity or quality.

Originality comes from a coherent material vocabulary and event grammar, not adding a puzzle mechanic or imitating Doom samples. ### Stage 0 — scope and input gate

The current RGB-only video fails the capture requirement. Correcting these is a proposed prerequisite, not work completed by this research report. An external WAV audition can assess source/edit quality while these runtime prerequisites are pending; it cannot establish the in-game result.

Keep the variables distinguishable. C/A/B source and processing comparisons all use correctly located source onsets, the same level-matched playback path, accepted shot cadence, variant order, spatial/background settings and capture chain. First compare those candidates with the existing mixer path **unchanged across candidates**. Then replay the identical exports/event sequence through the proposed corrected transient-start path, again unchanged across C/A/B. This paired 3-candidate × 2-playback-path study separates material/processing preference from mixer repair; never let A receive the fix while B keeps the old ramp. The source masters/hashes stay identical between playback-path runs. Legacy incorrect trims may be a separate labeled fault demonstration, never a candidate or a hidden advantage. Final gameplay approval uses the corrected path only after the paired diagnostic establishes that it works. If runtime fixes/capture are unavailable, stop at external source review and mark gameplay approval pending.

Deliver one close-spread weapon in three candidates, six controlled source performances each. One dry/new armor impact family, one soft contact family and one enemy windup/release/interrupt/death family support the scene. Do not commission a complete arsenal until this passes.

Acquisition sheet per candidate:

- A: six medium/hard struck membrane or damped drum takes, six wood/clamp snaps, six crushed stone/debris gestures, six jaw/spring returns. Capture dry close mic plus optional room mic as separate files; physically quieter substitutes can be edited to imply force.
- B: six intact resonant vessel/cavity strikes, six safe ceramic/fragment contact recordings from a licensed pack, six foil/cloth tears, six shutter contacts; electrical character from original short noise/FM processing rather than dangerous electrical capture.
- Common: six stone, six sheet metal, six thick plate, six wood and six soft/damp contacts; three clearly performed enemy phrases per state, not one pitched scream reused for all. Inventory includes raw tails, not just tiny predesigned game bursts.

Record original foley at 48 kHz/24-bit, with peak headroom (initial target around -12 to -6 dBFS), slate object/mic/distance/take, retain room tone and unedited source. These are acquisition proposals; this agent cannot substitute for a human performance or listening judgment. Use library audition samples to select source before buying. ### Stage 1 — edit before processing

Manually locate six onsets from waveform and listening; record exact region/timing in manifest. Retain 1–3 ms pre-onset if necessary; no 0.5-second guessing. Trim click-free at end; do not fade away the initial crack. Preserve source at its native rate in raw folder, work at 48 kHz float. Mono check any stereo downmix before committing: choose the useful microphone channel if averaging cancels attack/body. Control processing: DC cleanup only if needed, gain, end fade. No resampling to retro bandwidth.

For A/B align attack layers within 0–3 ms as a starting hypothesis, then audition 0/3/8 ms offsets; incoherent multiple attacks can become a flam. Keep body layer 6–12 dB below attack peak initially, detailed debris 12–20 dB below; adjust by listening rather than automatic peak equality. Initial body release bands: precision 70–140 ms, spread 140–240 ms, area launch 120–220 ms. Attack should occupy approximately first 1–8 ms; a useful physical decay can extend beyond this. These are proposals, not Doom measurements.

Filter body only as needed: initial highpass 35–55 Hz for energy that small speakers cannot use; useful body 100–350 Hz, abrasive detail 1–4 kHz. Reject scooping all mids to obtain only sub + fizz. A uses damped real resonances; B tests resonant component at -18 to -10 dB relative to impulse and a 60–180 ms decay, with no long bell note. Optional saturation on body stem only, wet 10–30%; always compare bypass. Compressor only when necessary, initial ratio 1.5–2.5:1, attack 10–30 ms, release 60–140 ms, typically <=2–4 dB reduction. Do not apply a universal master compressor by habit. Limit for delivery safety, retain the unclipped prelimited master.

Recovery belongs at the animation's physical return marker, not embedded at an arbitrary fixed offset if fire cadence changes. Candidate dry close-spread tail may run 250–500 ms provided retrigger mix remains clear. Use a tight 150–250 ms alternative for fast cadence, rather than speeding a long shot randomly.

### Stage 2 — deliverables and matching

`raw/` originals with rights records; `edits/` selected regions; `stems/{attack,body,detail,recovery}/`; `masters/` float 48 kHz unprocessed/mixed masters; `runtime/` 44.1 kHz mono 16-bit PCM only for current engine compatibility; export manifest with hashes, onset, active window, duration, gain, processing version, peak/RMS and separately measured true peak if available. Raw/stems stay private when license restricts distribution; repo contains lawful runtime derivatives and provenance identifiers.

Level match active 0–250 ms windows first, then human loudness match at fixed monitor volume. Whole 800 ms RMS can reward long silence/tails unfairly. Keep native sample peak and true peak distinct. Initial delivery ceiling -1 dBTP after proper measurement; absence of an oversampling meter must be disclosed, not called certified true peak. Do not normalize each take independently to erase natural performance differences. Target close-spread body identity and audibility, not a prescribed short-clip LUFS number. Reference listening is gain-matched by reviewer, so candidates cannot win merely by louder playback.

### Stage 3 — bounded listening and gameplay audition

First blind randomized C/A/B one-shot and 20-shot maximum-rate sequences, same source variant order, impacts off, music off. Repeat with each candidate's dry recovery. User scores attack immediacy, body on small speakers, apparent mechanical plausibility, distinctness and fatigue; write a concrete reason for rejection. Stop a direction if its raw/stem playback is already poor; do not bury it in more processing.

Then one 45-second gameplay route with fixed replay/event order: quiet hard room → six missed shots → two armor contacts → three soft contacts → enemy tell during burst → interrupt → one projectile flight/contact → lethal collapse → quiet aftermath. Run music off and on. Use randomized labels and decode after notes. Test ordinary stereo headphones, desktop/laptop speakers at moderate volume, and mono; low-volume rerun establishes whether detail depends on loudness. User playtest is final preference, supplemented by 2–3 people initially to discover disagreement; these numbers are a practical sample, not a statistical claim.

Fail-fast questions: Is onset still softened only in-engine? Is the body present without subwoofer? Can player distinguish gunfire from contact? Does enemy windup survive their own burst? Does armor sound different from flesh with the screen hidden? Does repeated fire stay readable? Is fatigue worse after 20 shots? Does C outperform both identities? If yes to the last, adopt C's source discipline and reconsider material direction before expanding.

Proposed gate: user calls a candidate a clear improvement in gameplay and wants to keep firing it; reviewer can identify armor/soft contact and the dangerous windup consistently; no clipping/dropout or unexpected limiter pumping in fixed stress sequence. Suggested 80% cue recognition across shuffled examples is a hypothesis to calibrate, not a universal industry threshold. A marginal improvement or an attractive one-shot alone does not greenlight 100 more assets.

## Event and mixer contract for later implementation

Authoritative simulation accepts/rejects actions; animation consumes the same accepted sequence and proposes physical submarkers that mechanics approves in the simulation schedule. Audio must never damage enemies or decide ammo state. Cross-read [role 12](Research2_Combat_Feedback.md) and [role 11](Research2_Weapon_Art.md): use their single packet fields `level_generation, simulation_tick, event_id, attack_id, source_entity_generation, target_or_surface_id, contact_index, position, incoming_direction, surface_normal_if_known, material_id, outcome, state_before/after, optional_cancelled_attack_id, seed`. Audio adds attachment/intensity metadata without creating a parallel event. One consumer handles audio; IDs prevent a render frame, replay or duplicate collision from firing twice.

**Canonical naming:** `ShotEmitted` is the single actual release packet. Role 12's `fire_commit/release` and this report's former `weapon.fire.accepted` mean that same packet and are aliases, not separately dispatched events. `input_accepted` begins an action and may have restrained mechanism motion/audio; it does not claim release or successful contact. Use role 12's `contact_blocked`, `damage_applied`, `damage_zero/immune`, `interrupt_committed`, `armor_break/state_change`, `death_committed`, `corpse_settled/removed` and `player_damage` outcomes verbatim. A contact can carry a material result plus a terminal response without manufacturing two hits.

**Muzzle ownership:** `muzzle_owner` selects the baked or external visual bright core; the one `ShotEmitted` packet schedules exactly one dry gun attack and bounded viewmodel impulse, regardless of the number of visual layers. Optional lamp/room return is a contribution of that same group, not another gunshot. No separate audio trigger comes from an atlas frame, overlaid flash or launch sprite.

**Shared timing specimen, not balance approval:** role 11/12's neutral close-fan sample emits `ShotEmitted` at 0 ms, outlet accent at 0–40 ms and `WeaponReady` at 600 ms. Audio attack belongs at that release timestamp; optional latch belongs at the actual ready boundary. Changes/cancellation follow role 03 mechanics. Projectile variant emits release at 0 but resolved contact occurs on its later collision. Do not bake a target-impact sound into the launch master. Useful audio onset aligns with the originating fire/contact/reaction timestamp; a 140–240 ms body tail need not equal the visual 0–40 ms muzzle or 80–100 ms contact accent. Record event-to-enqueue, enqueue-to-device and observed audio/video output latency on named hardware separately; requested SDL buffer size alone is not measured end-to-end latency.

**Warnings precede commitment:** an enemy windup must start on the authoritative pre-release attack-state transition, with a real reaction window owned by AI/mechanics. `ShotEmitted` or enemy release means the projectile/contact has committed and cannot double as its advance warning. If the current creature exposes only a release event, label it a release cue and mark the missing pre-release tell as an implementation dependency. Do not fake a warning by adding leading silence or moving the sound earlier than its state exists.

No global hit beep on every pellet; optional simple confirmation supports accessibility but cannot substitute for physical contacts.

Proposed **soft budgets**: 4 local weapon/recovery, 4 reserved critical danger/player signals, 10 impacts/voices, 4 world/travel, 2 UI. Reverb returns/music are bus work outside these effect-voice counts. Reserve policy must protect imminent windup and player damage; low-detail debris/remote tails lose first. Preserve dry onset. These are broad hypotheses tied to actual geometry/material, not accurate physical simulation claims. Occlusion gets a smoothed attenuation/filter and portal routing if supported; do not let a single ray mute every warning. Required reaction threats still need an intelligible cue when becoming attack-capable. Room identity requires quiet space between fights; long reverb everywhere defeats cadence.

## Mix, variation, accessibility and provenance

Variation: six performed takes per signature weapon as starting acquisition goal; shuffle bag with no immediate repeat, preserve comparable envelopes and recognizable identity. Very small +/-0.3–0.7 semitone/1 dB microvariation can be tested on secondary contacts; reject random attack timing/pitch that makes one weapon sound like three or obscures enemy role. Materials choose bounded banks, not arbitrary file lottery. Long bursts test accumulation/repetition at actual accepted cadence.

Keep attack/danger and music hierarchy authored. Initial music duck only 2–4 dB for imminent danger cues, 5–15 ms attack and 120–250 ms release; optional body-band competition reduction for gunfire rather than pumping entire score every shot. Confirm with music role agent. Never require blanket 12 dB ducking to rescue a deficient cue. Baked loud masters plus instant global ceiling are likely to erase contrast. Gain staging must leave headroom during the worst plausible multi-source event and verify headphone/speaker playback; amplitude alone is not weight.

Accessibility requirements: independent SFX/music/voice/ambience controls, mono toggle, restrained-dynamics mode with clear danger cues, subtitle/icon equivalents for notice/windup and directional aid, optional reduced piercing detail, no critical cue based only on very low bass, high pitch or panning. Reduced-dynamics mode is a distinct mix profile, not an always-on limiter. Quiet listening must preserve cue semantics. Default listening volume and hearing comfort are reviewer preferences; no claim that a digital dBFS ceiling defines a safe physical listening level.

Do not extract game audio or build derivative samples from copyrighted reference assets. Doom source publication and its age do not establish asset permission. Our original recording/library chain needs recorded author/source URL, download date, exact license copy/hash, source filename/hash, selected region, edits, derivative IDs and distribution permission.
