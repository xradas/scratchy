# Eyesore reference direction

Status: accepted direction for current production work.

## Identity boundary

Eye Sore stays an infernal descent shooter. New work can deepen its industrial hell setting with fresh demons, materials, spaces, and sound, but must not replace the game's world, mood, or core fantasy with an unrelated genre. Reference games are sources of craft lessons; their assets, signature sounds, and layouts are not production targets. This is the review check before integrating any new concept.

## What to learn from each reference

| Reference | Useful design lesson for Eyesore | Keep in check |
|---|---|---|
| Doom | Readable rooms, clear enemy tells, distinct weapon/enemy cues, movement through combat space | Copying assets, encounter layouts, or signature sounds |
| Wolfenstein 3D | Brief sounds and simple visual signals can communicate alert, doors, pickups, and danger | Treating its technical limits as a style requirement |
| DUSK | Strong location identity and generous space to learn movement under pressure | Retro texture noise without authored composition |
| Prodeus | Responsive music and deliberate retro presentation can coexist with modern tools | Treating visual effects or gore as a substitute for combat readability |
| Boltgun | Weapons and player movement feel weighty when animation, impact, material, and sound agree | Making every event louder or heavier until all contrast disappears |
| Dead Space | Light pools, spatial audio, environmental clues, and tension valleys make quiet spaces active | Slow survival pacing, constant darkness, and surprise spawns that undermine fair arena combat |

Dead Space's developers describe the remake's Intensity Director as combining authored event recipes across audio, lighting, fog, steam, and enemy spawning, with peaks separated by calmer intervals. The useful adaptation is a small, authored encounter director: compose readable combat peaks, then give players short recovery and navigation beats. Use optional ambient variation to enrich a route, not to interrupt movement or silently punish forward play. [EA: Intensity Director](https://www.ea.com/technology/news/inside-dead-space-4-the-intensity-director)

Dead Space also ties combat feedback to visible creature damage; Motive describes peeling and dismemberment as communicating damage as well as looking dramatic. For Eyesore, the transferable principle is state legibility: hits should visibly change a creature's pose, armor, or material response, at the renderer's actual pixel size. It does not require gore or a high-detail damage system. [EA: Remaking a Classic](https://www.ea.com/inside-ea/news/inside-dead-space-1-remaking-a-classic)

## Direction to test

Eyesore should stay a fast, forward-moving arena shooter with strong weapon identity and enemy silhouettes. Add Dead Space's light-and-sound tension selectively in approaches, transitions, and aftermaths; add Boltgun's physical weight to impacts and movement; use Wolfenstein's cue clarity and Doom's spatial combat readability. The combination is a hypothesis to test in one authored level slice, not a license to blend every reference into every room.

## Review rule

For each proposal, require one reference lesson, one concrete Eyesore adaptation, and one playtest question. Reject proposals that only add a list of props, enemy variants, audio files, effects, or room counts without showing how they change player decisions or feedback. Keep new research briefs short enough to review together; record links and decisions here instead of repeating the same reference summary in every specialist document.
