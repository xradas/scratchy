## Two original composition identities and a quiet control

| Aspect | A — Cut-paper propulsion | B — Long-line resistance | C — Quiet musical control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Musical promise | A tight, physical groove with clipped questions and unexpectedly spacious answers; nimble rather than frantic. | Broad, stubborn phrases push against a walking pulse; tension accumulates over a line rather than machine-gun subdivisions. | Recognizable pitched fragments and intentional rests let the world and weapons take attention. |
| Tempo trial | 128 quarter-note BPM, 4/4; straight subdivision, selected 3+3+2 accents inside a bar. | 96 quarter-note BPM, 4/4; half/double-time perceived energy through arrangement, no live tempo ramp. | Same 128 BPM grid as A for matched transition comparison; sparse attacks make it feel less driven. |
| Rhythm | Dry drum kit, muted picked bass, hand-muted strings; alternating busy bar and response bar. No constant cymbal wash. | Sparse low drums, sustained bowed electric guitar/bass, slow contrapuntal movement; peak grows through register/voice tension. No permanent sub drone. | Two or three short pitched events per phrase, mostly rests; no combat drum stem. |
| Harmonic trial | Narrow modal collection, pedal with occasional changing bass note; contrast by voicing/rhythm. Composer invents the contour. | Two related tonal centers linked by common tones, slowly changing suspended intervals; peaks can stay unresolved. | Stable dyad and one original answer contour; few changes, no placeholder foley bed. |
| Timbre/performance | Bass/drums plus one dry chamber voice (bass clarinet or muted viola), with limited precise distortion on a separate sustained voice. Human groove and articulation are audible production goals. | Bowed/plucked electric ensemble plus low piano/rounded mallet punctuation, controlled feedback only in tails. Performer shapes attack and sustain rather than adding a stock riser. | One convincing recorded or licensed instrument, supported by a restrained pitched texture; realistic phrasing required. |
| Motif rule | Two-bar question + two-bar answer; repeated intervals/rhythm identify it, answer moves between ensemble voices. | Eight-bar arc: rise, hold, incomplete descent; shortened two-bar fragment for discovery, full line reserved for meaningful pressure. | Four-bar sparse statement, next four bars leave space; retain an identifiable contour rather than random notes. |
| Peak | Tighter counterline and forceful broad accents, with holes reserved for weapon/warning peaks. | Register opens and harmony presses closer; drum subdivision doubles for one phrase then returns. | No louder combat mode; one slightly fuller phrase may acknowledge a major beat. |
| Recovery | Remove driving voice, leave answer at wider spacing, then allow genuine silence. | Let line descend incomplete, sustain its decay while rhythmic layer exits. | Let next phrase finish or omit it; silence is an authored choice. |
| Main risk | Chamber voice could become quirky/jaunty or percussive groove compete with repeated shots. | Sustained distortion can reproduce generic heaviness and mask bass/body cues; slow line could feel passive. | Underproduction can sound like a weak comparison; it must receive equal performance/editing care. |

## Bounded adaptive plan

First implementation candidate is **horizontal state cues**, not unlimited additive layering: Entry/Exploration, Combat, Peak and Recovery, plus an exit bridge. Teach uses a quieter combat arrangement; transition returns to exploration unless next scene changes identity. Only one active core arrangement, an optional current motif voice, and one outgoing tail coexist. A quiet control needs only one arrangement plus silence. A linear level loop is a valuable low-complexity comparison before adaptive playback.

Encounter authoring owns `engaged`, `pressure_peak`, `danger_resolved`, `left_encounter` and `exit_committed`. Danger resolution considers live attacks/projectiles and relevant chase, not only a zero enemy count. Music must not reveal unseen enemies: engagement can authorize a combat arrangement after perceptible confrontation, not distant activation behind a wall. Music cannot promise safety through an unresolved threat. Backtracking into an active encounter returns to combat; it does not replay the entry reveal.

Proposed hysteresis: enter combat on a real engagement; hold the current score state for at least one phrase except stop/death/exit. Recovery requests wait for 2–4s of continuously resolved danger, then go to the next approved musical boundary. Peak requires authored beat permission and at least one phrase between peak entries; player damage alone does not generate a peak. Those numbers are test hypotheses. A new engagement cancels pending recovery immediately and requests the next combat handoff; mandatory warnings play immediately regardless of the score grid.

Variation is authored and bounded: at most two compatible phrase variants in the first slice, selected once per phrase from seeded state; no repeated motif in every beat. Difficulty changes AI/resources, not score loudness automatically. Low health does not add a panic metronome that mimics the attack tell. At most one late-story transformation of the motif in the slice, triggered by a real authored clue/reveal. Optional O can use silence or current exploration without a unique secret fanfare. No infinite intensity meter required.

### Musical clock versus exact simulation events

Proposed request packet: scene/encounter IDs, event ID, simulation tick/time, target music state, reason, generation, requested earliest/latest handoff, selected cue/variant and cancellation token. Audio maps simulation timestamp to its output sample clock; transport has its own bar/beat phase and sample-accurate loop schedule. BPM/time signature/tempo-map metadata belongs to the cue. Fixed sample positions are authored at export; avoid rounding every bar independently and accumulating drift. All simultaneous stems share sample zero and length.

Gameplay `enemy.tell_begin`, `ShotEmitted`, `ContactResolved`, `InterruptCommitted` and `DeathCommitted` retain their exact semantic timing. They never wait for music. A score transition may wait for its next beat/bar/phrase, while selective music ducking can begin at the cue's scheduled output onset. Log latency; a sample callback timing claim is not measured speaker timing. A warning trigger can change immediate bus gain, but must not start an unrelated ominous phrase after it has already expired.

If the next phrase boundary exceeds the state change's permitted delay, use a composer-authored beat/bar exit or short bridge; no arbitrary cut through sustained pitches. Prototype proposal: combat entry maximum one bar, resolved release maximum one bar after its hysteresis hold, exit/death fade independent of bar. Review whether these windows feel responsive. Urgent combat events remain readable in that delay because SFX/hazard cues have priority. A static transition worksheet lists source, destination, allowable cut points, bridge, tail, and worst-case wait for every edge.

Pause freezes musical transport, scheduled requests and active gameplay voices after a short click-prevention fade; UI can use its own bus. Resume continues phase and state, fading audibility back without restarting the phrase. Mute continues transport as a user preference; pause is different. Death cancels gameplay score requests and either fades to silence or one later approved defeat fragment; avoid replaying a prolonged fanfare per attempt. Checkpoint restores encounter state/generation and a declared stable cue entry, not half a stale tail; exact music phase persistence is optional and explicit. Full restart selects the entry cue. Reset increments generation so queued prior-scene triggers cannot sound afterwards.

For a scratch packet, export A/B as rendered stereo full mixes plus **three compatible stems maximum**: rhythmic foundation, sustained/harmonic voice, and motif/answer. A/B may use fewer. A fourth optional file is a transition/tail, not a permanently added intensity layer. This export partition supports diagnosis; do not assume all stems must play together or use middleware because they exist. Avoid shared nonlinear mastering across stems that makes the recombined export differ from its full mix; supply a recombination check and state any shared processing. Peak may replace a stem/arrangement instead of adding another.

Proposed buses: Music, World ambience, Weapons, Threat/combat, Player/UI, optional Dialogue; shared master with conservative safety protection. Room reverb belongs to role 14/mixer contract; nondiegetic music is ordinarily not passed through each changing room zone. Music's own recorded/creative reverb is in stems or a clearly owned return, never duplicate world return. Diegetic music, if later needed, is an explicit positional emitter with a separate policy, not every score track.

Composer masters: 24-bit PCM WAV, stereo, one agreed sample rate (44.1k or 48k after engine choice), aligned stems at identical full sample length; runtime codec is a later engineering decision. Preserve mono compatibility rather than forcing mono. Provide uncompressed reference full mix. If compressed runtime files are chosen, verify decoded length, gapless loop behavior and codec delay on the actual decoder. No sample-rate reduction required for retro character.

Each cue manifest declares tempo/meter, cue ID, shared origin, intro range, loop start/end sample positions (end exclusive), compatible exit points, tail range, variant compatibility, gain reference, provenance/version and hashes. Export a full cycle containing pre-roll/tail for review plus a separate runtime region; do not confuse file length with loop period. Music-changing scene tails route to a bounded tail voice and stop on generation/reset; no accumulation after repeated transitions.

## Mix, fatigue and accessible playback review

Use one fixed approved/dry SFX control first. Follow role 13's corrected-onset/mixer questions before judging whether score masks weight. Establish matched *perceived* full-mix listening level for A/B/C, record chosen gains and measured integrated/short-term loudness/peak/crest information, then keep music-to-SFX gain fixed across map variants. Peak loudness alone is insufficient. Quiet control's intentional rests are real; do not normalize a sparse track until its isolated notes leap over the combat mix.

Arrangement should leave frequency/time space before resorting to ducking: do not put continuous percussion across every shot onset or sustained bass across every weapon body. Initial selective duck hypothesis: music attenuates 2–4dB around a required warning, with short 5–15ms attack, hold matching useful cue, 120–250ms release. Measure onset/recognition and audible pumping; this is not a production standard. Simultaneous warnings merge attenuation envelopes with bounded minimum gain rather than stacking independent -6dB cuts. Reserve Threat priority in the mixer; ducks do not fix dropped voices. Avoid ducking every ordinary shot by default; compare one fixed 2–3dB weapon/body accommodation only if arrangement fails. Role 12's VFX timestamps do not dictate musical decay length.

Review in headphones, stereo speakers, mono sum and a named small speaker. Confirm motif identity and rhythm at quiet volume; neither relies only on sub-bass or wide antiphase material. Compare stereo and mono level without silently compensating cancellation; document phase/width edits. Required tactical cues survive full music, low dynamic range/night mix and music-off; the accessibility mode's gain changes should be published. Independent music/effects/dialogue sliders, music-off, preserved gameplay meaning in muted mode, no essential threat encoded by pitch alone. Avoid full-band surprise volume spikes as a substitute for tension.

Fatigue packet: first-play 3-minute route, six repeated combat phrases, a 10-minute variable-dwell pass and three checkpoint retries. Ask about irritation, remembered phrase, urge to lower music, perceived shot weight, missed warnings, sense of release and repetitive transition fatigue. Compare state churn counts; if a short engagement repeatedly forces bridges, revise hysteresis or author quieter continuity. Check idle exploration for 90s, prolonged peak for 60s, bypassed peak, recovery interrupted after 1s, two boundary crossings within a bar, pause mid-tail, rapid mute/unmute, death during pending transition and scene exit under fire. These are future review cases; none were run here.

## Cross-review with world audio: shared ownership resolution

Read the completed `Research2_World_Audio.md` and directly discussed ownership with its specialist. The existing furnace bed is world/ambience material for audit, not a score stem. Ambience owns localized world loops/beds and geometry-controlled acoustic returns; score owns its nondiegetic arrangement and intentional creative reverb.
