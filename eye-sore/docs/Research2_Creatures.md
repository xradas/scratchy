# Eyesore round 2: creature sprites and animation

**Role 10 · GPT-6.1 Sol / medium · 3 October 2026.** Research and proposals for critique. No sprites generated, production files changed, game launched or tests run. Inspection of image metadata is an audit, not asset validation.

## Decision

Prove two readable enemies and their complete action cycles before ordering a roster. Current source sheets have substantial detail but share red/black armor, bright faces, round shoulder hardware and flames; all four types share animation timing. More detail or more generated sheets would multiply inconsistency. A new design must change body plan, motion, surface treatment and production method together.

Compare a neutral two-role control against one of two original production systems: **Cut-paper pursuit**, grounded human bodies treated as broad print shapes; **Jointed clay beasts**, deliberately nonhuman articulated ceramic forms with animal motion. Both are unapproved, and neither extends the Glass Choir, Kiln Wretch or current infernal armor. They do not select a story, engine or art direction. The human system is compatible with the Route Crew behavior proposal; the clay system can use the same simple role verbs without adopting the Returned Routine's replay mechanic.

**Recommendation:** start with the control and one side-view/attack study from each system. Choose through gameplay-size contact sheets and a moving encounter. The clay system supplies the larger body-plan departure, but has more animation and fiction risk. Do not quietly choose it because its names sound unusual.

## Reference research

### Doom: resource contract rather than monster imitation

**RF:** `r_things.c` names sprite resources through a four-character actor code, frame letter and rotation digit. Rotation 0 uses one patch for every direction; rotational frames require eight entries. One patch may map to a mirrored second entry. Renderer chooses direction relative to actor angle and uses patch offsets plus world position. It distinguishes fullbright frames from ordinary light tables. [id Software `r_things.c`](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM/blob/master/linuxdoom-1.10/r_things.c).

**I:** direction completeness, intentional symmetry and stable placement are production requirements. **P:** validate every declared state/direction and give all attack tells valid side/rear views. Use fullbright for a limited tell/effect, not the whole body. Eight directions are a sensible first comparison, not proof that eight always suffice for free-look vertical scenes. Original monster assets are unnecessary for this lesson.

### Wolfenstein 3D: economically different states

**RF:** `WL_DEF.H` enumerates eight guard standing facings and eight entries for each of four walk poses, but separate small pain/death/shoot sets. `WL_ACT2.C` describes state actions/timing independently of sprite-number declarations. [id Software `WL_DEF.H`](https://github.com/id-Software/wolf3d/blob/master/WOLFSRC/WL_DEF.H), [`WL_ACT2.C`](https://github.com/id-Software/wolf3d/blob/master/WOLFSRC/WL_ACT2.C).

**I:** spending frames where action changes is valuable; equal frame counts for every state are unnecessary. **P:** budget recognizable preparation, shot and recovery before extra walk smoothness. Its limited directional shooting is a historical tradeoff, not a requirement for Eyesore's infighting and elevation. No guard imagery/uniform/signage is imported.

### Boltgun: a reproducible source for many facings

**RF:** Auroch lead designer Grant Stewart explains sculpting/texturing enemies, rigging and animating them, then rendering animation images from eight camera directions. These become Unreal flipbooks with authored damage, sound and projectile moments. [Auroch developer account on PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/04/11/warhammer-40-000-boltgun-releases-may-23-new-gameplay-details-revealed/).

**I:** shared source geometry offers consistency across angles and revisions; the final pixel result still needs art judgment. **P:** compare a small rig-rendered clay actor with a hand-painted human before choosing the production route. The article establishes its pipeline, not exact resolution, shader, renderer cost or staffing. Its blanket history of Doom maquettes is not repeated as a claim about every Doom sprite.

### DUSK and Prodeus: presentation claims with limits

**RF:** DUSK's official listing emphasizes three authored episodes and 1990s FPS inspiration. Prodeus describes modern rendering combined with older-hardware aesthetics, a handcrafted campaign and an editor. [DUSK developer/publisher listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/519860/DUSK/), [Prodeus developer listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/964800/Prodeus/).

**I:** coherence and authored context matter more than adding a pixel filter. **P:** test enemies against distinct location surfaces and through motion, rather than only on black. These pages do not establish their exact enemy sprite pipeline, number of frames or internal animation events; those are unknown in this report. Their marketing is not a measured readability study.

### Dead Space: body condition as information

**RF:** Motive's character/animation team describe preserving enemy silhouettes and a layered damage/peeling system that exposes enemy condition; they also discuss the tracking cost of moving Leapers among several enemies. [Motive developer interview](https://www.ea.com/able/news/inside-dead-space-2-new-necromorph-nightmare).

**I:** an enemy's body can communicate tactical condition, but motion complexity competes with tracking. **P:** one readable stagger/break state can convey impact without importing dismemberment or multiple damage texture layers. Reject ceiling-leapers as first production enemies until actual height/navigation exists. A cosmetic chip must not imply a weak point or easier interrupt unless gameplay makes that true.

## Shared neutral control: two roles before taxonomy

**P:** role names below describe experiments, not fiction. Use player body height H and body diameter D from the movement lead; H is not the current 1.42 camera anchor. No new health/damage values selected here. Behavior lead owns damage, commitment and interruption. **Close pursuer maps exactly to Cross; single-shot lane holder maps exactly to Hold.** Hold remains at its authored anchor rather than performing the optional approach animation. Its windup snapshots a target point and its projectile releases only through the agreed clear-shot trace. Cross uses direct seek, then a short bounded low-speed committed movement along the observed heading and a stopped recovery. It gains no homing, flank, leap or fast-charge guarantee from its art. Cross's tell must show the lowered/braced body and forward lane before movement; Hold's tell must show the tool/throat axis and braced pose before the projectile. Both use the behavior lead's shared LOS/commit/event rules, with exactly matched timings in comparisons.

## Alternative A: Cut-paper pursuit

**P:** an original human adversary system with physically grounded weight and print-like surfaces. Bodies are clothed in large angular wrapped panels with exposed ordinary hands/boots and off-center tool masses. Clothing folds are few deliberate shapes, not noise. No cult robe, gas mask, skull icon, glossy power armor or religious mark. Names/occupation are storyteller decisions.

- Close pursuer: a low lunging human with wide split shoulder wrap and paired forearm pads. Knees/elbows establish the wedge; no horn/crown. Tool mass is carried close until the windup separates forearms. Body lowered by posture, not an inexplicably short person. Human-only scenario remains possible.
- Lane holder: long narrow torso, one diagonally wrapped hip panel, one square projecting tool held away from the body. Preparation unfolds tool axis into a horizontal beam of shape; no sniper glint, colored laser or magical sigil needed.
- Palette: warm gray paper, blue-black fabric and one limited dull rust tell panel; mid-value skin/hand clusters keep human action legible. On pale architecture choose a dark outer mass; on dark architecture preserve a broad light chest/arm plane. This is two lighting treatments of the same body, not a different role by palette.
- Animation: hand-author rough silhouette keys over a consistent proportion/turnaround model; use held keys and stepped inbetweens. Start with mass and feet, then tool/cloth follow-through. Cloth may move after the shot but cannot obscure commit pose. Recoveries visibly absorb weight.
- Damage: broad fold displacement and a brief separate dust/fiber effect. A persistent torn panel optional after actual stagger; no full crimson flash needed. Death separates tool from hands and collapses human support plausibly; optional violence intensity is the effects lead's scope.
- Production system: hand-painted RGBA cells plus shared eight-view mannequin guide; fixed pixel grid, named bones/contact guides and palette ramps. Strong authored clusters, low rig cost; expensive revisions across directions and risk of perspective drift.

**Challenge to Civic Daylight:** dark upright humans against bright planes could make every enemy a black cutout. Give each role a different distribution of gaps, horizontal width and moving mass. **Challenge to Painted Eclipse:** silhouette-on-silhouette can lose depth; retain body planes and floor contact, reject universal neon rims. This direction's strength is clarity/fast iteration; weakness is potentially bland masked workwear or human-only shooter sameness. Reject it if testers describe only “guards with different weapons.”

## Alternative B: Jointed clay beasts

**P:** original nonhuman forms built from broad fired-clay masses and visible flexible dark joints. They are constructed animals rather than machines wearing human armor or anatomy with added eyes. No lenses, choir pipes, exposed organs, magic runes, glowing central wound, horns or flying flame tails. Fiction explaining them is intentionally undecided; material can support crafted, industrial, domestic or uncanny worlds.

- Close pursuer: broad flattened chest supported by four short splayed legs, front pair noticeably heavier; triangular side profile with no conventional face. A dark hinge seam visibly opens across the front body before a straight shove. Foot placement and chassis lean establish weight; avoid dog/boar reskin with decorative ceramic texture.
- Lane holder: two-legged high pelvis with a curved counterweight tail and a side-jointed tubular throat, producing a narrow vertical silhouette with a lateral attack axis. During preparation, throat unfolds outward; recovery folds it into the body. No weapon bolted onto a humanoid shoulder. Its projectile origin and its visible mouth/throat align.
- Palette: pale mineral glaze, desaturated ochre rough edge, dark joint seams; single muted cool insert reserved for the extended attack surface. Gloss represented by a few fixed broad stepped highlights. No environment-reflection sparkle per pixel, automatic emissive crack web or saturated rainbow glaze.
- Animation: low-poly or simple sculpted original rigs rendered through fixed orthographic cameras. Splayed foot contact and counterweight motion define every pose. Render uncluttered diffuse/value pass, then pixel-clean key planes; retain integer frame bounds and manifest. Stable source rig permits eight combat angles and consistent damage condition. This is a proposed pipeline, not existing capability.
- Damage: a large chip at the same location across views after actual stagger/break; exposed dark interior is a material fact, not automatically bonus damage. Separate fragments clear quickly. Death breaks the main support mass into two or three broad forms so the corpse is unlike an idle crouch; do not simulate hundreds of shards.

**Challenge to Civic Daylight:** pale creatures against pale walls need dark outer joints and a large mid-value saddle plane, not a bright outline. **Challenge to Painted Eclipse:** clay cannot become generic neon stone golems; preserve rough/glazed surface contrast and animal support rhythm under subdued light. Risk: art pipeline could consume more time than the game, and manufactured animals might need too much lore. A clay rig failing to produce obvious low-resolution shape is a pipeline failure; do not rescue it through more texture detail.

## Animation, facing and combat contract

Everything here is **P** and must be co-reviewed by AI, engine, lighting/audio and effects leads.

`ActorDefinition` should own world body/support dimensions, target volume, render canvas/pivot, material/light mode and animation set. `Clip` owns frame durations, direction policy and declared events. Simulation owns state, attack ID, event crossing and damage. Rendering samples simulation state; dropping a render frame cannot cancel a shot or repeat one.

Starter authored poses per role: idle 2, approach 4, telegraph 2, release 1, recovery 2, pain 2, death 5 plus static corpse (reuse death final). At eight facings, this is **18×8=144 images per role**, without gib/extra damage tiers. It is a budgeting example, not a required quota. The current 48/type is smaller largely because combat is single-view. Two complete enemies are already 288 images; an eight-enemy roster at this baseline would be 1,152 before effects. Symmetric mirror reuse may reduce authored files but only if body/tools/condition are genuinely symmetric. Never mirror asymmetric chip/tool just to fill missing angles.

- First rough study: one idle/approach/tell/release/recovery/death key in front, side, back; label unfinished directions. Full eight angles needed for a moving gameplay verdict. Single-view prototype cannot approve directional production.
- Eight yaw directions at 45° spacing; define direction zero and handedness in manifest. Add small sector hysteresis as a proposed way to avoid jitter at boundaries, after checking direction lag under circling. Do not interpolate silhouettes into blurred ghosts.
- Horizontal billboard remains plausible for flat/pitch-light play. Test actual elevated viewing angle first. One optional three-band elevation set triples cost; choose it only if pitched side views visibly fail and engine supports it. Otherwise constrain authored sightlines or use a runtime mesh for that class.
- Idle has support/breathing motion without attack tell. Walk stride length and simulation speed should agree; no identical sliding foot motion across pursuit speeds. Freeze locomotion clock when stationary. Root motion is visual only in baseline; attack shove distance belongs to AI.
- Telegraph changes at least two large masses; release includes one recoil/body impulse; recovery remains visually distinct from preparation. The attacker’s facing/aim is fixed at commitment within behavior-defined turn bounds.
- Named `tell_begin`, `commit`, `release`, `active_end`, `recover_end`, `foot_contact`, `pain`, `death_impact` events carry actor/attack ID, simulation tick and world origin. Dispatch crossed intervals once, including multi-event low-framerate crossings. Event sound/effect does not own damage.
- Nonlethal hit feedback independent of interruption. Cosmetic flinch can be additive/local; full pain clip only if interrupt contract allows. Interrupt cancels release explicitly; lethal state prevents delayed projectile events. Pause/reset remove pending events and restore seeded timing.
- Stable collider during ordinary animation. Intentional crouch or broken support state may change it through a named gameplay state; collider transition audited with traces and floor support. Do not derive targeting volume from transparent bounding boxes or decorative sparks.
- Projectile/muzzle origins stored in actor space and verified against each rendered facing; large canvas/tool tip is not the whole hittable enemy. Hit volumes stay inside meaningful body masses with reasonable aim forgiveness; none at detached particles.

## Export and renderer requirements

**P:** Keep editable original art/rig separate from baked runtime atlas. Use integer cell dimensions, named state/direction/frame, source provenance, canvas bounds and explicit pivot/support coordinates. Use true transparent RGBA PNG export, no baked checkerboard, black matte or background guessing. If a chosen native path still needs BMP, preserve a supported alpha representation and establish actual SDL import behavior; the current flat-BMP/key path is insufficient. Pipeline must reject a missing declared file rather than silently render invisible actors.

Do not independently trim/rescale each pose. Trim only during atlas packing with stored offsets; hold shared actor scale and support guides. A lunge can reach outside standing canvas, but its pivot does not follow the resulting bounding box. Apply nearest scaling from a chosen logical art grid; no per-frame automatic palette reduction or morphology that shaves neutral hands/joints. Padding/extrusion around atlas cells prevents sampling bleed, with alpha-aware RGB edges for any optional filtering. Choose straight or premultiplied alpha once and match blend mode; do not mix.

The current 384×256 full RGBA uploads cost 384×256×4×192 ≈ **72 MiB** for enemy texels alone, excluding driver overhead and any copies. This is an estimate from code/dimensions, not VRAM profiling. For proposed 144-image sets, 128×128 RGBA is 9 MiB per enemy; 192×192 is 20.25 MiB; two actors ~18/40.5 MiB. Packed occupancy may reduce wasted transparent area; mipmaps/compression/copies change totals. Many small texture binds can cost more than texel memory. Use atlas batching only after defining cutout depth/sorting; no performance promises from file count.

Compare three routes: hand-painted sheets (fast silhouette exploration, expensive consistent angles); rig-to-sprite render and cleanup (strong angle consistency, upfront rig/render tooling and repeated bakes); runtime low-poly mesh with stepped animation (cheap new views/dynamic light, potentially loses crisp authored sprite look). Pixelating a mesh does not automatically recreate sprite style. Prefer a small shared rig only after the same actor has been viewed in motion and at far distances. Image generation can suggest one approved body design later; independently generated sheets do not supply reliable pivots, event timing or anatomical consistency.

## Lighting and screen-pixel visibility

**P:** Use broad diffuse/body value planes modulated by authored area/local lighting and separately limited tell accents. Avoid painted hard directional shadows that contradict world light. Contact cue/shadow indicates foot support; hovering enemies require different support/target rules. Lighting lead should choose light bands/tint response, contrast fallback and flash constraints. Optional accessibility silhouette treatment must be consistent across enemy roles and avoid hiding damage condition.

For a flat centered target, approximate projected canvas height as `canvas_world_height × framebuffer_height / (2 × tan(vertical_FOV/2) × distance)`. At the current 60° vertical FOV and a hypothetical 2-unit canvas, 720-high rendering gives ~125 px at 10 units, ~62 at 20, ~42 at 30. These are geometry estimates, not observed creature heights: transparent padding means the visible body occupies less than the canvas. At 360-high internal render, those become ~62/31/21 px. Far sprite detail budgets must be based on **visible opaque body pixels**, not PNG width or final display upscaling.

Use the lighting lead's shared initial bands: near 64–128 body px, tactical 32–63, preview 16–31 **at internal scene resolution**. These supersede this report's earlier independent bands. Hypotheses for human review; preview is not approval for lethal attacks requiring precise tell identification. Sample additional larger near views where needed, but do not create a competing definition of tactical range. At far range, tell should move a large contour or a contiguous 3–5 logical-pixel mass, not require a one-pixel eye or color-only panel. Cropping a sheet to 32 pixels tests silhouette approximately; actual projection, occlusion, temporal sampling, lighting and display need gameplay captures.

Capture the identical actor IDs, positions, clip timestamps, FOV, window size, render scale, light and camera path in control/A/B. Take raw internal-resolution PNG and user-display screenshot; record settings in filenames/manifest. Include actual normal/far engagement frames plus grayscale/color-vision simulation and a camera moving laterally, not only enlarged contact sheets. Avoid post-capture brightening; wrong light must remain evident.

## Cross-review: lighting controls and visual comparison order

**P, revision after coordinator review.** Use explicit labels **light C / light A / light B** for neutral fill / broad architectural light / graphic plane staging, and **visual N / visual Print / visual Clay** for neutral geometry / Cut-paper pursuit / Jointed clay beasts. Letters do not mean the same alternatives in both disciplines.

The two visual systems intentionally **do not use identical full silhouettes or anatomy**: a human pursuer and a four-legged clay pursuer cannot be matched limb-for-limb without removing the body-plan proposal. Accordingly a complete Print-versus-Clay comparison is a **whole creature-system preference/readability comparison**, not evidence that ceramic or print materials alone caused the result. Keep the role-defining gross envelope, projected body height/width, center/support pivot, permanent body/tool gap, tell displacement footprint, event timing, attack path and collider the same within agreed tolerances; record residual differences. Do not force identical poses onto incompatible supports. If one system requires faster movement, a bigger target, longer tell or a new attack to look good, test that separately and label the changed variable.

Use three ordered comparisons rather than a full uncontrolled matrix:

1. **Light comparison:** use visual N's exact same Hold/Cross sprites, silhouettes, poses, base body ramps, alpha, masks and clip timestamps across light C/A/B. Follow the lighting lead's first surface-only pass with neutral gray actor/material, **emissive off and actor fill identical**. Approximate mean visible neutral-surface luminance matching once in V2, then lock values for V1/V3/V4. This isolates broad gradients versus plateaus; no actor recoloring or per-view exposure rescue.
2. **Material/style comparison:** take one identical approved role outline and pose set as a carrier. Repaint that same carrier into Print and Clay material treatments with the same base body luminance bands and identical emissive mask. Here outline/anatomy/pivot/action cannot change; only palette hue, broad cluster treatment and surface character may differ. This modest study measures material/style treatment, **not** whether a quadruped beats a human. Identical silhouettes are mandatory in this stage.
3. **Complete creature-system comparison:** apply each proposal's original body plan to the same Hold/Cross behavior and selected light system. Use matched gross role envelopes and timings but the appropriate support/anatomical motion. Collect role/tell recognition, target tracking and original-identity preference separately; attribute differences to the combined system. Only after this stage should a selected creature be taken through all light systems in an actor-crossing clip to review their different actor-response ramps.
