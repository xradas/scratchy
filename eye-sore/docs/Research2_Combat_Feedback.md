# Eyesore round 2: combat feedback and visual effects

**Role 12 · GPT-6.1 Sol / medium · 3 October 2026.** Research and proposed comparisons only. No code, effects, assets, builds or playtests produced. Native FPS is the working target. Engine, creature, arsenal, story and art choices remain provisional. Weapon-art report appeared during this review and its event/alpha/mask proposals have been cross-read; all its designs remain provisional.

## Decision

First establish truthful outcomes, then compare **C: compact physical contact**, **A: pressure and recoil**, and **B: cut and separation** on identical mechanics. C may win. A gives impacts mass through a short directional compression and sparse displaced matter; B gives them identity through clean planar cuts and brief separation of surface layers. Neither requires neon magic, screen-filling blood, a camera jolt on every pellet, or enemies with health bars. Target posture and committed state remain the primary evidence that an attack stopped or an enemy died.

The strongest current problem is semantic: the prototype equates weapon-colored sparks and a green crosshair with a successful hit, has one shared impact slot, and gives no distinct response for contact, effective damage, interruption and death. More particles would make that ambiguity louder. Weapon mass should come from coordinated shot pose, confirmed contact, appropriate target reaction and audio, while control and tracking stay reliable.

**RF: DUSK.** New Blood's official description emphasizes fast retro FPS play and varied settings. **RF: Prodeus.** The official listing describes retro aesthetics with modern rendering and dismemberment/gore. ## Authoritative event contract

**P:** simulation produces outcomes at its selected fixed step; render samples them. No tick frequency is selected here. Each record has `level_generation, simulation_tick, event_id, attack_id, source_entity_generation, target_or_surface_id, contact_index, position, incoming_direction, surface_normal_if_known, material_id, outcome, state_before/after, optional_cancelled_attack_id, seed`. Origin/normal must come from a shared trace, not sprite canvas corners. Missing normal uses a compact nondirectional fallback, never a guessed ricochet. Audio, VFX, pose and optional HUD consume the same event independently. Failed cosmetic allocation cannot undo damage.

| Event/outcome | Owner and tick | Consumer behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `input_accepted` | Weapon action begins at tick T | Optional mechanism motion. No hit, ammo success or muzzle claim yet. |
| `fire_commit/release` | Mechanics atomically commits resource and ray/projectile at named marker | One muzzle group, shot sound and viewmodel impulse. `muzzle_owner` chooses baked flash OR external sprite for its bright core. Optional light is a separate bounded contribution of this group. |
| `projectile_spawn/travel` | Simulation projectile ID, position/velocity/lifetime | Core tracks the actual trajectory, optionally interpolated. No contact preview. Trail is attached cosmetic history and never a second projectile. |
| `contact_blocked` | Shared trace stops on wall/armor/solid boundary | Material contact and optional denial shape; no positive damage notch. Ricochet only if mechanics actually spawn a redirected projectile. |
| `damage_applied` | Damage resolver after mitigation/immunity | One contact result per hit target. Optional crosshair notch only for local player's confirmed effective damage. Flesh/machine/clay response follows actual target class. |
| `damage_zero/immune` | Resolver reports zero damage with reason | Compact hard-contact/denial response if visible, distinguish from miss. Do not present this as success or expose hidden weak points. |
| `interrupt_committed` | AI cancels the identified active attack | Clear role-specific posture break; active tell stops; one short contact accent. Cosmetic flinch alone does not emit this event. |
| `armor_break/state_change` | Mechanics changes named real rule | Persistent broad plate/tool/support variant if approved, plus one local break. No decorative chip promises a new vulnerability. |
| `death_committed` | HP terminal/state commit | Stop tell/attack events, begin death pose, terminal contact accent once. Future hits follow explicit corpse rules. |
| `corpse_settled/removed` | Actor lifecycle/support event | Quiet static corpse, optional small floor settling accent; no attack-like glow. Removal follows level policy, not particle timeout. |
| `player_damage` | Player damage resolver | Stable edge-sector cue toward known source/impact, health update; optional single bounded motion response. No source cue if the source is unknown, no through-wall tracking marker. |

**Predicted versus confirmed:** current single-player runtime has no networking prediction contract. Local input animation may precede release; it communicates action, never a hit. A muzzle effect proves release, not contact. A moving projectile hitting later confirms later. If a future network/replay layer predicts contact, keep speculative traces cosmetic and neutral, with unique reconciliation IDs; reserve success/kill/interrupt cues for confirmed outcomes. A correction must remove speculative traces without replaying muzzle or hiding an accepted hit. Never blink a success notch when the cursor merely crosses an enemy.

Pellets retain `attack_id + contact_index`; damage can aggregate per target while preserving world contacts. Emit at most one substantial target accent and one optional confirmation per target/attack, with sparse grouped wall marks. For same-tick damage/death, choose terminal response plus material contact; suppress competing pain/stagger clips. For death plus interrupt, death wins and attack cancellation remains in the event log. Catch-up updates dispatch markers crossed in `(previous_tick,current_tick]` once; pausing freezes simulation and cosmetic lifetimes; reset advances generation, flushes events and pools.

## Three visual grammars

All three use the same actor poses, attack timings, contact placement, damage rules, material classes and optional crosshair convention. Creature Print/Clay and environment A/B are compatibility choices, not automatic pairing requirements.

| State | C: compact physical contact | A: pressure and recoil | B: cut and separation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Release | Small opaque muzzle core, quick two-pose mechanism return | Short asymmetric cone suggesting directed pressure; brief metal/tool compression and rebound | Thin segmented opening at muzzle, retracts immediately; avoid crosshair-like central glyph |
| World contact | 2–4 coarse flecks and small mark | Flattened contact wedge oriented to normal, flecks travel outward then settle | One shallow broken plane or short edge cut, 1–3 broad slivers peel away |
| Effective target damage | Local contact + restrained cosmetic body/tool reaction | Local compression/displacement with obvious recovery to active posture | Local seam splits briefly, closes if no state change; retain original body contour |
| Real interrupt | Distinct lower/open tool posture, no glowing badge | Whole action mass drops along impact direction, stops before it conceals lower threat | Attack axis visibly separates/folds down; two displaced planes stop the tell |
| Real condition change | Authored broad condition variant | Bent support/plate changes only when that gameplay rule changes | Persistent missing/shifted broad insert only for committed change |
| Death | Clear support collapse and quiet corpse | Heavy support drop plus one small floor impulse, no universal blast | Two or three main masses lose alignment and settle, preserving dead-body identity |

**A identity:** short directional material displacement, weighted recoil on weapon and target, sparse chalk/enamel/cloth response. Avoid expanding spherical fire rings and long dust plumes. It can suit daylight construction or dim graphic scenes; pale dust against pale walls must become mid-value flecks, not additive glow. Human targets show cloth/fabric motion and support collapse; clay shows coarse chips; neither requires anatomical gore. Main risk is fake knockback: if physical actor movement is absent, body/tool displacement must stay inside the cosmetic envelope and return quickly. No telegraphed dodge window changes because of a cosmetic squash.

**B identity:** planar tear/cut/offset composed in coarse clusters rather than radial bursts. It can pair with layer/cut materials or contrast with assembly surfaces. It should read as local contact, not a floating UI stamp, supernatural portal, or a route glyph that teaches a puzzle. No full-body freeze, outline or negative-color flash. Main risk is that separation looks like armor break every time; only actual state changes persist. Human clothing/tool surfaces and clay shell seams use distinct local fragments, not identical paper confetti.

**C identity:** dependable, quiet, quick. It must receive original art and confident timing so it is a fair quality control, not deliberately dull. If A/B cost more and offer no useful identification/weight preference, retain C and invest in weapon motion/audio.

## Material and enemy classes

Material impact class comes from role 09's manifest; enemy material class is explicit gameplay data. Surface appearance does not create penetration or armor. Incoming direction/normal defines a hemisphere for outgoing flecks; grazes can produce tangential flecks, but spark direction is not a ricochet promise. Static decal aligns with actual plane plus a small stable offset; moving surfaces use local transform and entity generation. Door resets/removal remove attached decals. Never paint a impact across an entire doorway or beyond the struck receiver polygon.

| Class | P: compact identity | Semantic exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral/concrete | 2–4 matte granular flecks, short dent | Dust never sustained smoke cover |
| Coated metal | 1–3 narrow pale sparks plus darker coating flecks | Sparks alone do not indicate blocked damage or bounce |
| Ceramic/clay | 1–3 angular broad chips, matte exposed patch | Persistent chip only if cosmetic condition or real break is explicitly classified |
| Fabric/print composite | Short fold/edge tear, subdued fiber flecks | No legible symbol suddenly implying new state |
| Soft organic | Small local darker fluid/contact mass and pose response | No full-screen spray, realistic anatomical exposure or inherited Doom gore assets |
| Machine armor | Hard local response, real deformation only on state commit | Damage-zero versus effective damage uses outcome convention, not spark brightness |
| Decorative glass/unknown | Conservative compact puff; glass initially solid per material role | No falling shards/penetration until gameplay has that contract |

Blood/gore is an optional content setting. Low setting substitutes local material-colored contact and the same pose/crosshair/state cues; kill, interrupt and corpse recognition must work identically. No blood marks on route labels, key shape or hazard edges. Corpse texture/pose loses attack aperture, raised tool and standing support. Corpses start nonblocking in the control; another blocking design belongs to AI/level mechanics and requires its own visibly solid class. Debris is cosmetic, has no damaging collider and does not resemble pickups. Keep persistent gameplay condition marks separate from expiring combat decals; cleanup must never heal armor or erase state.

## Timing, coverage and comfort hypotheses

Use role 08/10's **near 64–128, tactical 32–63, preview 16–31 opaque body pixels at internal resolution**, and the shared 640×360 comparison framebuffer. Final display pixels are not the unit. No one-pixel marker is asked to carry a mandatory state. Collect recognition and tracking in movement, not only peak stills.

Initial P budget: release core 25–40 ms; contact primary accent 50–100 ms; small residual flecks 120–250 ms; brief cosmetic target displacement 60–120 ms; interrupt/death posture duration owned by AI/art, not capped by this cosmetic table. Fast weapons coalesce overlapping cosmetic cores instead of producing strobe-like independent peaks. These are design starting bands, not photosensitive-safety certification. Do not change a genuine attack window to fit VFX duration.

At tactical range begin with a contact primary footprint no wider than one-third visible actor width, with no opaque particle cloud crossing the whole torso/tell. Aggregate bright muzzle/impact coverage starts below 3% of the full scene, with no effect owning the central 10% width × 10% height aim region for more than 50 ms per shot. The geometry of these flags is a review aid, not guaranteed readability; measure both luminance contribution and occluded silhouette/tell pixels. A few bright pixels can be more disruptive than a larger matte patch. Per-shot violations and continuous overlapping firing intervals both matter. Do not shrink world actors or move the camera to make an effect pass. At near point-blank contact, clip/simplify cosmetic expansion; preserve mechanics and local feedback.

**P maximum consecutive masking flag:** investigate any cosmetic loss of a living threat's critical tell over 50 ms, and any recurrent disappearance of the next threat at a normal impact. These thresholds may prove too strict or too loose; compare C and user recognition. Genuine cover occlusion is logged separately. No automatic enemy-outline or effect cutout around every enemy is added as an invisible scoring rescue.

Camera control: control has zero simulation-camera kick, roll, blur and hit-stop; weight starts in viewmodel/target motion. Optional presentation kick is decoupled from aim/trace, bounded and independently tested; if adding it requires target reacquisition, prefer weapon motion. Never stop global simulation for impact. Reduced motion removes kick/roll/bob and reduces cosmetic displacement without changing shot/tell timing. Reduced flash removes local additive/light contribution and uses a matte muzzle opening/compact contact. No full-screen white flash, rapid strobe, automatic exposure pulse or damage color wash. Record comfort complaints and stop/revise the particular presentation; numeric budgets do not prove safety.

Muted audio: release must show mechanism/muzzle action, incoming projectiles retain directional core, interruption visibly stops tell, death visibly loses support, and player damage uses a modest perimeter cue plus health change. Color cues redundantly use outline/shape/posture: effective hit briefly closes two short crosshair notches, zero-damage contact spreads two short notches outward if assist is enabled, kill uses target death rather than another color. Optional assist can be disabled, and it never reports unseen enemy hits through a wall by default. Crosshair remains readable over pale/dark surfaces via bounded dark backing; accessible palette presets apply consistently and do not turn every impact fluorescent.

## Rendering, alpha and performance contract

P order: opaque geometry → protected surface decals → actor/projectile opaque cutout cores with correct depth → depth-tested sorted translucent residue → bounded additive accents → independent viewmodel/muzzle layer → stable HUD/accessibility cues. Material C/lighting C comparisons initially turn additive and transient light off. Emissive is a semantic mask distinct from body, never “all bright pixels.” No depth-disabled world contacts, tracers through walls or additive body sprites. Actor/projectile cutout may write depth; translucent residue tests depth and does not write it. Receiver decals require polygon/local clipping and avoid z fighting. Transparent effects use explicit straight-alpha PNG and matching blend factors, or one documented premultiplied pipeline; never mix. Black-key alpha and painted matte halos are import failures. Atlas gutters and mip/nearest policy follow the shared renderer, checked at logical pixel scales.

The current OpenGL immediate-mode path has no custom effect shader or color-managed output, so soft particles, per-pixel particle occlusion or sophisticated emission are future capabilities. Lowest-cost test uses textured cutout contact sprites/sparse geometry and explicit layer/state restoration. A late light or effect pass must restore blend, depth write/test, alpha test, texture and lighting state, rather than leak current function state into HUD/world. Pose render may interpolate, damage resolution does not depend on sampled frame.

P initial stress capacities, to be replaced by measured target hardware: 32 simultaneous contact groups, 128 cosmetic flecks, 64 static decals in the encounter, 8 cosmetic trail histories with bounded samples. These are not projectile/actor gameplay caps and must not limit damage. Reserve a confirmation/state-response path independent of decorative particles. Priorities: live attack tell and projectile core > real interrupt/death pose > confirmed contact > small material fragments > residual trails/decals. Cosmetic overflow drops oldest low-priority residue deterministically; log counts. Never drop a projectile or use the single last-impact slot as “optimization.” High-priority state poses are actor data, not pooled particles.

Pool keys include generation/event ID; prewarm shared atlases for the slice, no per-hit file IO or allocations in the hot path. Record CPU/GPU times, draw groups, overdraw, dropped groups and total effect memory on named hardware. Use role 08's selected frame target and 95th/99th percentile comparisons; there is no justified universal millisecond cap yet. Texture storage arithmetic is distinct from frame cost. Shorten transparent coverage before increasing particle capacity. Cleanup/reset/replay must be deterministic for selected seeds and must not erase actor condition.

## Failure cases and production gate

A target flinches yet releases: ordinary hit response was falsely presented as interrupt; revise outcome/pain animation. A chipped shell looks weak but rules unchanged: replace persistent accent or label that design as a real mechanic in weapons/AI. Additive pulse erases another enemy: lower/remove contribution and revise local shape; do not add a universal outline. A pellet hit shows one giant explosion and one green marker: keep attack grouping but separate truthful target outcomes. A far bolt appears wider than a door: constrain readability accent and preserve a legible physical core. A stopped rocket still has trail moving into the wall: terminate trajectory history correctly. Unknown armor invokes guessed ricochet: display contact, not a new path. Death at low render rate skips its final pose: simulation enters corpse regardless of sampled frames. Invisible off-screen hit flashes confirmation: default policy limits assist to appropriately visible local outcome; positional audio remains governed by its occlusion contract.

Self-critique: A could feel like conventional industrial shooting with tasteful dust; its identity depends on coordinated directional compression and material choice, not the label. B risks obscure graphic gimmicks and armor-break confusion; its restrained transient-versus-persistent distinction must pass muted identification. C risks being underproduced as a weak comparison; equal craft and truthful timing are mandatory. None addresses the user's bad audio by itself. This role requires a conversation with the audio team on one event's release/contact/reaction timing and masking, and with weapon art on muzzle ownership and lower-screen occupancy before any assets are commissioned.

Small production gate: approve shared outcome vocabulary, one neutral actor's contact/interrupt/death pose, and one viewmodel's release marker. Compare those in movement. Only after this evidence expand per-material accents, alternate creature systems and arsenal-specific terminal treatments. No campaign asset count is a success criterion.

## Coordinator revision: B is a transient contact construction, not shell damage

The coordinator's objection is valid: a briefly split body seam on every effective hit could imply that armor was damaged, or simply repeat Dead Space's weakening language in miniature. **Revise B's ordinary-hit row:** the actor's surface, outline, condition sprite, material layers and hit volume stay unchanged. Instead a compact contact construction forms immediately *outside* the struck plane: two or three flat matte tiles, split by negative space, offset tangentially in opposite directions and withdraw into the contact point. They are clearly temporary rendered accents, not fragments peeled from a body. They contain no body texture, exposed tissue/interior or glowing wound, and are never retained as a target decal. Real break conditions still use their separate authored state variant. If the graphic accent is interpreted as a weak-point mark or lingering vulnerability, B fails and is simplified or dropped.

**Concrete neutral experiment (P):** retain identical contact position, body pose, damage, actor material, first-display time, total duration and approximately matched peak perceived prominence for C/A/B. First match rendered contribution in one diagnostic view, then lock all views; do not quietly increase B brightness. C is one compact stationary irregular contact stamp plus 2 flecks. A is a tapered normal-directed compression wedge followed by outward fleck travel; its silhouette grows away from contact and decays. B is two matte 2–4 logical-pixel-wide tiles at tactical range, separated by one logical-pixel gap, with no enclosing ring: at contact they are adjacent, during the next 30–50 ms they move 2–3 logical pixels in opposite tangential directions, then retract/fade by 80–100 ms. These pixel/time values are initial hypotheses, not guaranteed recognition. The B tiles are surface-oriented with bounded camera-facing compensation only where the plane would collapse to a line; that compensation is shared/documented and does not move the actual contact. Whole-effect footprint remains inside the existing one-third-body-width starting bound. No persistent crack, displaced actor plane or full-body color inversion is created.

This makes the visible distinction **stationary localized contact (C), normal-direction expansion/rebound (A), tangential separation/retraction (B)**. Do not use radial particles in B or tangential tile motion in A during this isolated pass. Capture at 30/60/120 rendered FPS and the shared logical resolution: if B's separation/retraction becomes a one-frame speck, or its silhouette is indistinguishable from A/C, treat the distinction as unproven. Allowing a longer effect would be a separate documented experiment, constrained by tell masks, not a silent fix. Passing the distinction only proves the three samples differ; useful weight/readability/preference still requires player feedback. No distinctive behavior is purchased by adding a stun, hit-stop, weak state or longer attack window.

### Resolver owns material; visual pixels never decide it

The shared trace identifies a receiver surface/entity and contact part. The authoritative contact/damage resolver looks up that receiver's declared `impact_material_id`/part mapping from the approved material or actor definition; it also resolves `effective_damage`, immunity/block reason and genuine state transitions. Its finalized result supplies those fields to VFX and audio. VFX cannot infer a metal hit from shiny paint, clay from palette, armor from sparks or damage from sprite transparency. A purely cosmetic authoring override may choose an accent variant inside the same declared class, but cannot change the resolved class, attenuation, penetration or damage result. Unknown/missing material uses the shared neutral fallback and authoring diagnostic in both sound and picture. A collider spanning multiple material parts must expose an authored part ID or deliberately use its default; no per-pixel material sampling is presumed. Cosmetic chips never write back a new material or weak state. Armor breaking can change subsequent resolver material only through the gameplay state commit, not because a previous impact rendered a sliver.
